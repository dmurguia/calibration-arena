# Copy review: AfterQuery and the first Calibrated draft

Reviewed and revised 22 September 2026, following founder feedback that the site was verbose, generic and unclear about the company.

## Diagnosis

The first draft imitated the *rhythm* of a technology landing page without consistently communicating the product. Short sentences were not the same as clear sentences. Words such as work, standard, judgment, practice, material and handoff appeared repeatedly without adding much information.

The opening described a desired agent outcome before identifying what Calibrated sells. The rest of the page repeatedly explained our attitude toward quality. A reader had to infer whether the company supplied data, ran model training, sold an agent product or offered consulting.

## What the reference does better

[AfterQuery's homepage](https://www.afterquery.com/) begins with “We teach machines how experts think.” It gives the company an active role, then explains the data offering and names product categories. Its calls to action lead to a purchase conversation or supporting evidence.

[Its enterprise page](https://www.afterquery.com/solutions) describes identifiable services and what the customer receives at each stage. The nouns carry the explanation: datasets, benchmarks, models and agents. That makes the offer easier to scan than a sequence of abstract promises.

AfterQuery also uses broad rhetoric and assertive performance claims. It is not uniformly concise. The useful lesson is to identify the service and explain it with concrete actions. Its scale, customer evidence and end-to-end capabilities are not evidence about Calibrated and were not adopted.

## Specific weaknesses and edits

| First draft | Why it failed | Revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agents that meet the standard of your work. | Could describe an agent application, a consulting firm, an evaluation tool or a training vendor. It did not identify the company. | AI training for professional work. The next sentence explicitly names training data, evaluations and RL environments. |
| Make the standard part of the system. | Abstract nouns replace the service. The section repeats what the hero already tries to say. | Remove the section. |
| The ingredients of better work. | Metaphor obscures an otherwise useful list of deliverables. | What we build. |
| A standard you can test. / Examples worth learning from. / A place to practice. | Three slogans force readers to decode the product categories. | Evaluations / Training data / RL environments. |
| Work at the same table. / A defined body of work. | Decorative names add a second label to already understandable partnership models. | Embedded / Managed, followed by the actual division of work. |
| A clear question. A concrete handoff. | Repeated two-part cadence, with no specific artifact in either half. | Explicit descriptions of test cases, rubrics, datasets and environments on the enterprise page. |
| Care in the details. Evidence in the result. | A quality assertion unsupported by additional information. | Remove the section. Keep the brand philosophy on the company page and the hero artwork caption. |

The full accounting demonstration was also too dominant on the company homepage. It is now a short introduction with a link to the functioning example on the approach page. Its provenance and validation status remain beside the example.

## Current opening

**AI training for professional work.**

Calibrated builds training data, evaluations and reinforcement learning environments with domain experts. For companies developing AI agents.

Actions: **Get data** and **For enterprises**. The approved graphite artwork appears alongside the text on desktop and directly beneath it on mobile.

The rest of the homepage has three service rows, a short Arena introduction, the two partnership models and one closing invitation. The revised copy does not claim customers, scale, benchmark results or a deployed training platform.

## What the original JEV run established

The model compared two drafts that shared most of their copy. Its persona rubric emphasized relevance, scope, differentiation and next steps. It did not compare either draft with AfterQuery or directly test whether the wording was boilerplate. A relative preference under that rubric was not evidence that the winning draft clearly explained the company.

Only the enterprise-buyer choice was stable across the two candidate orders. Five other persona choices changed. That was too weak a basis for recommending the original copy without a harder editorial review.

The original inputs and results are preserved. `/review/` now displays the actual selected-answer probabilities, full JSON responses and download links. It explicitly identifies them as **first-draft results**. The new copy has not been relabeled as JEV-approved, and no new JEV result is claimed.
