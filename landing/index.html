The problemWhy an arena, not a leaderboard

A benchmark score says how far. It doesn’t say where, or why.

Labs and vertical vendors now buy work product, not trivia: reconcile the accounts, draft the redline, code the encounter. The public benchmarks show how far models still have to go; the best of them clear only about half the criteria on expert-authored accounting tasks. But one number can’t tell a team which criteria they lose, whether a miss is a cutoff error or a policy misread, or what data would fix it. Our answer is an arena: licensed CPAs grading live model output against the public benchmarks and against closed-universe close work, criterion by criterion, by judges whose own accuracy is measured.

CompanyCalibrated Co.

A watchmaker never trusted a movement because it looked right.

He put it on a timing rig, measured the error against a known reference, and adjusted until the instrument agreed. That is the whole idea here. The AI industry has built a large workforce of experts to teach and grade models, and almost none of it is on a timing rig.

Calibrated builds the rig. Calibration Arena is the public face: practitioners blind-judge AI work product, hidden checks score the judges, and the boards show who holds up. Corsac is the eval engine underneath: a graded library and a pipeline that produces closed-universe tasks with fact-anchored rubrics on demand. The panel that comes out of the arena, with its measured record, is what we sell to labs and vertical vendors.

We are starting in accounting because the ledger is the oldest instrument we have for measuring work, and because you cannot hand-wave a debit. Founding design partners are being selected now.
